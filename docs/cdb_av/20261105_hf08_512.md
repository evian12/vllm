## Comparisons and interpretation

- The same research baseline with threshold 0.4 and KV reuse ON measured **138.204 ± 1.160 tok/s** over three 512-request runs. The current 0.8 + low-log configuration is **16.81%** higher, but this 512 comparison changes both threshold and logging. Isolated 128-request A/B experiments found the threshold-0.8 improvement independently (0.4: 136.349; 0.8: 158.488 tok/s), and low-log ON added about 2.0% on its own branch (OFF: 160.045; ON: 163.272 tok/s). These small experiments do not establish exact 512-run attribution.
- Native AR measured **174.663 tok/s** and native speculative K=1 measured **186.995 tok/s** on the same prompt construction, GPU/backend and service limits, but each has only one 512-request run. Current CDB remains **7.57% below AR** and **13.67% below native K=1** by these single-run points. Do not claim CDB is faster than either.
- In detailed 128-request logs, threshold 0.4 yielded approximately 53% Target forwards without candidate verification; threshold 0.8 reduced that to approximately 24%. Both configurations filled most Target batches. Thus the improvement is associated with fewer low-value Draft candidates **and** a greater fraction of Target rows doing verification, not just less Draft GPU work. A 128-request timing probe attributed most diagnostic wall time to Draft and Target forward calls; its instrumentation altered call counts and TPS, so it is unsuitable for a production throughput comparison.
- Reuse remains enabled and its safety assertions were not relaxed. Strict generated-token equivalence and GPU kernel-level attribution remain open. This checkpoint is a reproducible performance improvement, **not** complete CDB-AV performance acceptance.
